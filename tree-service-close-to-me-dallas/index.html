When you type tree service close to me Dallas into your phone, you'll get a long list of names. Some are real local crews. Some are lead sellers who pass your job along. Knowing the difference saves you time and sometimes a lot of grief.

This guide walks through what to ask, what to watch for and how to get your yard ready.

Before you hire tree service near you

Start with the basics. Ask who will actually show up at your house, and whether they're the same people who gave the estimate. A company that sends its own crew is easier to hold accountable than one that subcontracts.

Ask if someone with arborist credentials looks at the tree. An ISA Certified Arborist can tell whether a tree needs light pruning, support or removal. That matters because cutting the wrong way can hurt a healthy tree for years.

Ask about insurance too. Trees fall on fences, roofs and cars. You want the crew's coverage in place before a saw starts.

A short checklist for comparing tree companies

Use this when you're talking to two or three companies:

  • Do they carry insurance, and will they say so plainly?
  • Will a certified arborist review the work?
  • Is the estimate free and written down?
  • Does the estimate say what happens to the limbs, chips and stump?
  • Will they explain why a tree should come down, not just say it should?
  • Can they come out after a storm if something falls?

If a company dodges two or three of these, move on.

Mistakes to avoid with a tree service close to me Dallas search

The biggest one is picking the first ad you see. Ads aren't a sign of skill. Read what the company says about its own work and call to see who answers.

Another mistake is paying for removal when pruning would do. A good crew only recommends taking a tree out when it's dead, dying or a hazard to your house. If someone pushes removal on a tree with green leaves and solid wood, get a second opinion.

Watch out for topping, where the crown is chopped flat. It looks quick and cheap, but it stresses the tree and leads to weak, fast regrowth.

Last, don't climb a ladder with a chainsaw. Every storm season people get hurt trying to save a few hundred dollars on a limb near a power line.

What to prepare before the crew arrives

You don't need to do much. A little prep makes the day go smoother.

  • Move cars out of the driveway and away from the work area.
  • Clear patio furniture, toys and planters from under the tree.
  • Tell the crew about sprinkler lines, pet doors and gates.
  • Keep dogs inside.
  • Take a few photos of the tree and any damage, especially after a storm.

It also helps to jot down what bothers you. Branches over the roof, a leaning trunk or a stump you keep tripping on all help the estimator understand the job.

When to call a pro

Call right away if a tree or limb is on the house, on a fence or leaning after high winds. Call soon if you see dead branches, mushrooms at the base, cracks in the trunk or limbs hanging over the roofline.

Questions people ask about tree service close to me Dallas

How do I know a tree company near me is legitimate?

Ask if they're insured, who will be on site and whether a certified arborist reviews the work. Get the estimate in writing so you know what's included, including cleanup.

Do I need to be home when the crew works?

Not always, but it helps to be there for the estimate so you can point out what you want done. On the work day, just make sure the crew can reach the tree and any gates are unlocked.
